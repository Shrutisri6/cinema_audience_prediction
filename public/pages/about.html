Behind The Scenes
How This Works

The Forecast Logic

Each theater carries a historical baseline — its median nightly audience. The backend multiplies that baseline by three factors: a day-of-week effect (weekends and Fridays trend higher), a seasonal wave across the year, and a booking-demand signal built from the online/walk-in sliders on the Forecast page. The combined ratio is clipped to a sane range, mirroring how the notebook clips its target_ratio before training.

This mirrors the notebook's overall approach: instead of predicting raw audience counts directly, the winning LightGBM model learns log(audience / baseline) — a ratio relative to each theater's own history — which handles the huge scale differences between a small single-screen theater and a large multiplex.

Model Bake-Off

The notebook trained and compared five regressors: Ridge, Lasso, GradientBoosting, XGBoost, and LightGBM. LightGBM came out on top and was retrained on the full dataset for the final submission. The scores shown on the Forecast page are illustrative stand-ins for that comparison — run the notebook on the actual competition files to get real R² figures.

What's Real vs. Simulated

Real: your account, login session, password hashing, and everything on your Forecast Log — all served by this Express backend and stored in a local database file.

Simulated: the eight theaters and their baselines are realistic sample data, not the actual Cinema Audience Forecasting competition dataset, since no dataset or trained model was supplied for this build.

Adding Your Own Theaters

The Add Theater page lets you register a theater with its own name, area, type, baseline nightly audience, and a custom weekly demand pattern (how busy each night of the week tends to be, from 0.3× quiet up to 2.5× packed). Once submitted, it's saved to the database and shows up in the Forecast page's theater list for everyone — you can forecast against it immediately, and remove it later from the Add Theater page if you created it.

Wiring In The Real Pipeline

To turn this into a genuine prediction service: train and save the notebook's final LightGBM model, stand up a small inference endpoint that applies the same feature engineering, and swap the sample day-of-week / seasonal formula in server.js for a call to that endpoint. Then replace the sample theater list with real theaters and their actual historical stats.
